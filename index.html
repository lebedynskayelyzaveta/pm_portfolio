The context

As part of the production studio team, I was responsible for the visual production workstream of a major international concert event. My specific mandate was to build the team from scratch — sourcing freelance 3D artists and animators from an open talent pool, negotiating rates, and managing the full delivery pipeline from brief to final video assets shown on stage.

My role

I served as production PM and team lead: assembling the freelance team, setting up the workflow, owning all internal communication between artists and the studio, managing scope and revision cycles, tracking milestones, and ensuring quality standards across every deliverable. I was the single coordination point for the entire visual output.

Key challenges

  • Building a team of 13 from a freelance pool — no existing relationships, rates to negotiate from scratch
  • Distributed team across multiple timezones — Ukraine, Europe, and Asia
  • Multiple scope change requests throughout production — each requiring negotiation and replanning
  • No pre-existing shared workflow — production system built from zero at kickoff
  • First collaboration between most team members — trust and rhythm had to be established quickly

How I managed it

  • Sourced and onboarded 13 freelancers, negotiating individual rates and setting clear contractual expectations
  • Built an async-first workflow with written standups and shared task tracking to bridge timezone gaps
  • Managed scope changes formally — assessed impact, negotiated additional fees where justified, and replanned affected milestones
  • Maintained structured feedback cycles with the studio on a fixed schedule to prevent uncontrolled revision loops
  • Acted as sole communication bridge between studio and creative team throughout the project

Key takeaway

Managing a creative freelance team is a different discipline from managing a salaried one. Without formal authority, the PM's credibility comes entirely from clarity and consistency — clear briefs, fast decisions, and predictable communication. This project taught me to build those systems proactively, before problems arise, not in response to them.

What I'd do differently

I'd formalise a change request protocol from day one — a one-page document defining what triggers a scope conversation, how impact is assessed, and what the approval chain looks like. Managing scope informally works, but a written process removes the emotional weight from every negotiation and creates a paper trail that protects everyone.
